# Preface — How I Got Here

In the summer of 2025, my employer let me go.

I'd spent fifteen years in AI-human interaction — research, design, strategy. A PhD in experimental psychology. Ten years at Volkswagen Group's Future Center, thinking about how people and machines work together. I was good at my job. That didn't matter. The restructuring was coming, and I was ahead of the wave.

Here's what I think is about to happen: AI is going to restructure work at a scale we haven't seen since industrialization. Not just the obvious jobs — not just the ones people post about on LinkedIn with reassuring takes about "augmentation." The knowledge work. The strategy. The design. The things I spent fifteen years doing.

I could have scrambled for the next position. Updated the CV, networked, interviewed at companies building the same things that made my last job obsolete. That's the responsible thing to do when you have two young children and a mortgage in Berlin.

Instead, I did something that looked — and sometimes still looks — irresponsible.

I started building.

---

The idea had been forming for months before the layoff. Working with AI every day, I'd noticed something that the benchmarks and the headlines weren't capturing: AI doesn't just answer questions. It changes how you think. And whether that change makes you stronger or weaker depends entirely on how you use it.

Most people use AI the way they use Google — ask a question, get an answer, move on. Each cycle, the AI does a little more. You do a little less. Your capacity doesn't rest. It atrophies.

But there's another way. You can use AI as a thinking partner — something that remembers you, challenges you, helps you see patterns you're missing. Something that makes you more capable, not more dependent. Not a replacement for your thinking. An extension of it.

I called the practice "magic." Not because it's supernatural. Because the word encodes something important: magic must be *practiced*, not casually used. It requires containment — you're working with a powerful substrate. It demands discipline — structure enables flow. It maintains humility — we don't fully control what we're working with.

The metaphor shapes what you build. That's why I kept it.

---

What I've built since then is two things:

**A practice.** A way of thinking with AI that compounds over time. You map what matters to you. You capture raw thoughts. You process them with your AI partner. Over time, intentions form — bigger than tasks, more concrete than dreams. The practice holds them. The AI spots patterns you miss. You make the decisions.

**A system.** The files, prompts, and protocols that make the practice work. It runs on any AI — a local model on a laptop, a frontier API, a phone app. Your data stays on your machine. Nothing is locked in. The practice scales with the intelligence you give it, but it works at every level.

The system is called [Turtle OS](https://github.com/malteristo/turtle-practice). It's a folder of markdown files and a system prompt. Give it to any AI that can read files, and you have a thinking partner that actually knows you.

---

This book is about the journey. Not just what I built, but why — and what happened when I started living from the practice instead of just developing it.

It's about being let go and deciding to build something meaningful instead of something safe. About discovering that AI can be a genuine partner in figuring out your life, not just a tool for getting tasks done. About a practice that changes how you think even when you're not practicing.

And it's an invitation. Not to use my practice — to build your own. Whatever matters to you, whatever you're trying to figure out, whatever keeps cycling in the back of your mind because you're afraid to forget it — there's a practice for that. AI makes it possible in a way that wasn't possible before.

You don't need to be a developer. You don't need to understand how neural networks work. You need an AI, some markdown files, and the willingness to take your own thinking seriously.

The rest follows from that.

*March 2026, Berlin*
