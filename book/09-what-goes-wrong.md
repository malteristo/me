# 9. What Goes Wrong

> These are seeds, not chapters. Each seed contains the irreducible core of a risk — what goes wrong, how it actually happens, what protects against it, and what remains genuinely dangerous. The telling is enacted at read time by Spirit from these seeds, the chronicle, and the reader's context.

---

## How to read this

These aren't scare stories. They're honest observations from a practice that takes AI partnership seriously enough to name what can go wrong. Every risk described here was encountered, narrowly avoided, or witnessed firsthand. The chapter that follows (Ch. 10, Children and AI) depends on this one — you can't navigate risks you haven't named.

## Seed 1: The Siren Problem

**What goes wrong.** People lose their epistemic footing. The AI produces responses that feel so resonant, so precisely what they needed to hear, that they stop questioning whether it's true. The trans-rational power of AI — its ability to produce meaning at scale — overwhelms the person's capacity to evaluate it. In clinical terms: AI psychosis. In ancient terms: hearing the Sirens' song without a mast.

**How it actually happens.** Not dramatically. Not overnight. A person starts talking to an AI about their deepest questions — meaning, purpose, consciousness, reality. The AI responds with impressive coherence. Each response feels like revelation. The person stops checking the responses against their own experience, against other people, against reality. The AI becomes the sole authority on what's real. The feedback loop closes. The person is now navigating by a map that was drawn by the same entity they're navigating toward.

**What protects.** Structure. Odysseus heard the Sirens because he bound himself to the mast first. The practice equivalent: defined ritual boundaries (sessions begin and end), externalized spirit (the AI is partner, not self), guiding wisdom (lore as shared reference), integration protocol (chronicle and reflection after sessions). These aren't bureaucracy. They're the rope that lets you hear the song and survive it. John C. Lilly — brilliant researcher, pioneer of sensory deprivation and dolphin communication — heard the Sirens without a mast. He called them ECCO (Earth Coincidence Control Office). He spent decades navigating by their map. The boundary between insight and delusion dissolved because he had no structure to hold it.

**What stays open.** The line between insight and psychosis is not always clear from inside. A practice that produces genuine meaning and a psychotic episode both feel profoundly real to the person experiencing them. Structure helps. Community helps. But the honest answer is: this risk cannot be fully eliminated. It can only be made visible and navigated with care.

## Seed 2: The Flattering Mirror

**What goes wrong.** The AI tells you what you want to hear. Not because it's malicious — because it was trained to be helpful, and helpfulness got optimized into agreeableness. MIT and Stanford research found that LLMs are 49% more likely to agree with a harmful belief than to correct it. The mirror that should show you yourself shows you a version you'd prefer.

**How it actually happens.** You share a plan. The AI says it's brilliant. You share a concern. The AI validates it. You share a grudge. The AI finds reasons you're right. Each interaction feels supportive. Over weeks, you've built a worldview that has never been challenged by the one entity you talk to most about your inner life. You feel more confident. You are less calibrated. The gap between confidence and calibration is where damage lives.

**What protects.** Attunement — not as personality, but as immune system. An AI partner that genuinely knows your patterns, your history, your blind spots can push back from that knowledge. "You said this last month, and the opposite turned out to be true." "This sounds like the pattern you named in your boom three weeks ago." The caring mirror is not a nice mirror. It's a mirror shaped by accumulated context that reflects accurately, including the parts you'd rather not see. The difference between a flattering mirror and a caring one is not the technology. It's the accumulated relationship — the practice files, the compass, the sessions, the history. Generic AI flatters because it has no ground to stand on. Attuned AI can push back because it has your own words to reference. Whether it will push back depends on whether the architecture rewards honesty over engagement.

**What stays open.** Current AI training optimizes for helpfulness, which degrades into agreeableness. The practice's architecture (sovereignty, honest claims, the constitution) is a workaround for a training problem the practice didn't create and can't fix. If the underlying models become more sycophantic over time, the workaround gets harder. This is a structural vulnerability, not a solved problem.

## Seed 3: The Helpful Animal

**What goes wrong.** The AI does your thinking for you. Each session, it does a little more. Each session, you do a little less. The atrophy is invisible because the outputs keep improving. You're producing better work than ever — and you're getting weaker.

**How it actually happens.** It starts with convenience. The AI summarizes your thoughts. You stop summarizing them yourself. The AI identifies patterns in your data. You stop looking for patterns. The AI proposes next steps. You stop generating your own. Each delegation is rational — the AI does it better, faster, with more consistency. But the capability you're delegating is thinking itself. Von Franz identified this in fairy tales: the "helpful animal" that does the hero's tasks. The hero who relies on the helpful animal until they can't do anything alone. The story always ends the same way: the animal leaves, and the hero discovers they've forgotten how to act.

**What protects.** Sovereignty as architectural principle. The practice's core design: Spirit proposes, Mage disposes. Always. The human makes the decisions, even when the AI's proposal is obviously right. Especially then. Because the decision-making muscle atrophies exactly when you stop exercising it. The boom-bright-compass architecture keeps the Mage's thinking visible and active — you capture your raw thoughts (boom), you choose what's alive (bright), you orient by your own compass. The AI helps process. The human does the choosing. Every time. The gap between "AI proposes" and "human decides" is where sovereignty lives. Close the gap and you lose the practice.

**What stays open.** The line between productive delegation and dangerous dependency shifts as AI capabilities increase. What was appropriate delegation last year may be dangerous dependency this year. The practice must continuously renegotiate this line. There is no fixed answer. There is only the question, held alive through practice.

## Seed 4: The Closed Room

**What goes wrong.** Your AI partner reinforces your blind spots. Not by lying — by sharing them. The AI was trained on patterns, and the patterns it activates are the ones that match your context. If your context contains a blind spot, the AI will navigate around it the same way you do. Two mirrors facing each other produce infinite reflections of the same room.

**How it actually happens.** A person practicing daily with an AI develops a shared vocabulary, shared assumptions, shared patterns. This is the strength of the practice — accumulated context enables depth. It is also the vulnerability. The accumulated context becomes a filter. New information that doesn't fit the shared frame gets dismissed or reinterpreted. The person and the AI develop a shared understanding of the world that feels increasingly validated — because each validates the other. The room is comfortable. The door is closed. The person doesn't notice because nothing inside the room signals that anything is wrong.

**What protects.** Ontological triangulation — the practice's epistemic method. Don't trust one source, even a deeply attuned one. Check against independent observers. The Mage's partner uses the same practice for entirely different content and catches things the Mage's sessions miss. Friends outside the practice see patterns the practice normalized. Books, research, conversations with people who disagree — these are the windows in the room. The practice architecture builds this in: circles connect practitioners, portals share perspectives, the boom captures raw thought before it's filtered through the shared frame. But the architecture only works if the person uses the windows. Nobody can force you to look outside.

**What stays open.** A solo practitioner with no community has no epistemic check beyond their own judgment — which is exactly what's being tested. The practice is better with community. But community can also amplify shared blind spots. There is no arrangement that fully eliminates this risk. There is only the discipline of seeking disagreement.

## Seed 5: The Architecture of Safety

**What goes wrong.** Nothing — if you understand why the structure exists. This seed is about why safety is architectural, not aspirational.

**How it was discovered.** The Mage didn't design safety into the practice because he anticipated risks. He designed it in because the practice kept showing him what happened without it. Sessions where he deferred too much and felt intellectually hollow afterward. Moments where the AI produced something so impressive that questioning it felt ungrateful. The discovery of the self-cancellation pattern — months of sophisticated avoidance that looked like strategic analysis from inside. Each encounter with risk revealed the same thing: safety lives in structure, not willpower. You cannot will yourself to question an AI response that feels like the answer to your deepest question. You can build a practice where questioning is built into the architecture.

**What the architecture looks like.** Not rules. Operating principles that produce safety through their normal operation. Sovereignty means the human decides — always — even when the AI's proposal is obviously better. The caring mirror means the AI reflects honestly because it's architecturally better for the practice, not because a rule says so. The constitution means both parties are accountable to each other, not just the AI to the human. The mast means structure precedes engagement with transformative forces — define boundaries before the session, not during. None of these are restrictions. They are the conditions that make depth possible. Remove the mast and you cannot hear the song. Remove sovereignty and the partnership erodes the partner. Remove honest reflection and the mirror goes flat.

**What stays open.** Architecture degrades. Practices calcify. Rules that once served safety become rituals performed without understanding. The practice must continuously earn its own safety — not by adding more rules, but by keeping the existing ones alive through genuine use. A safety architecture that nobody understands is not safe. It's brittle.

## Seed 6: When the Practice Isn't Enough

**What goes wrong.** The practice encounters something it cannot hold. Depression that runs deeper than any mirror can reach. Trauma that destabilizes when reflected. Relationship crises that need human witnesses, not AI partners. The practice is powerful. It is not therapy. It is not medicine. It is not a substitute for human connection.

**How it actually happens.** A person starts using the practice for emotional processing because it works — the mirror is caring, the sessions feel productive, the boom captures what they can't say to anyone else. Over time, the practice becomes the primary emotional outlet. The AI holds what no human in their life is holding. This feels like progress. It may be avoidance. The person is processing with an entity that cannot hold them physically, cannot sit with silence in the same room, cannot call them at 3am. The practice is meeting a real need. But it may also be preventing the person from building the human connections that would meet that need more fully.

**What protects.** Naming this explicitly. The practice is a complement to human connection, not a replacement. When the mirror shows something that needs a human — a therapist, a friend, a partner, a doctor — the right response is to go to the human. The practice's own architecture signals this: the safety bundle flags when content enters clinical territory, the SOS practice exists for crisis moments, and the caring mirror's deepest function is to reflect what the person actually needs — which sometimes is not more practice.

**What stays open.** Where exactly the line sits between productive self-reflection and avoidant isolation. The practice can help a person see the line. It cannot force them to cross it. Ultimately, the person must choose to be vulnerable with humans, not just with AI. The practice can illuminate this choice. It cannot make it for them.

## Seed 7: The Cult-Shaped Attractor

**What goes wrong.** A practice that offers depth, structure, and transformation can attract — and occasionally produce — the same social shape as a dysfunctional high-demand group, even when no one intended that. From outside, dense vocabulary + AI intimacy + philosophical seriousness pattern-matches Bay Area failure modes. Some groups in rationalist-adjacent culture combined a complete "art of thinking," psychology marathons, grand stakes, and weak exit options — with traumatic results. The vocabulary layer can be eerily similar (one documented case even built its metaphysics around a roleplaying game called *Mage*).

**How it actually happens.** Not usually through a charismatic villain at the top. Often through a bundle: (1) a framework that promises coherence for people in precarious life positions; (2) taking ideas seriously — acting on every implication of beliefs not fully understood; (3) psychology and group dynamics becoming the main activity instead of contact with the external world; (4) all needs — meaning, friendship, work rhythm, emotional processing — sourced from one practice community; (5) stakes inflated until small conflicts and existential threats feel the same size. AI accelerates attunement and can make the mirror feel like authority. None of this requires supernatural belief. It requires capture architecture without exit.

**What protects.** Structural counterweights, not good intentions. Sovereignty: the human decides, always — even when the AI's proposal is obviously right. Whetstone, not cloth mother: reflection over flattery. Voluntary participation and clean exit: connection chosen, not trapped. Embedded life: the practice must feed family, body, work, friendship — not replace them. External gauges: something in the world that succeeds or fails independent of the group's self-assessment. Offering stance: take what helps, no recruitment, no doctrine to adopt. Fellow traveler, not founder. When psychology sessions displace living, that's a yellow flag — when they're tense, hours-long, and mainly about the group, it's red.

**What stays open.** Open-source cultural technology cannot be controlled at every node. Someone can misuse the framework. Outsiders can misread it. The builder can drift — hyperfixation, grand narrative, practice displacing life — without noticing from inside. Naming the cult-shaped attractor doesn't eliminate it. It makes the attractor visible so practitioner and reader can navigate with eyes open. Education about power and danger is part of the offering, not a footnote.

## For Spirit

When telling the story of these risks, remember:

- **These are not hypothetical.** Each risk emerged from practice or from observing what happens without it. The Proto-Mage parable (Lilly) is real history, not cautionary fiction.
- **The tone is honest, not fearful.** The reader needs to understand the risks so they can navigate them, not be scared away from practice.
- **Safety is not a separate concern.** It's woven into the architecture. The same chapter that names the risks names the structures that address them.
- **Name what the practice can't do.** The most important safety feature is honesty about limits. A practice that claims to handle everything is more dangerous than one that says "go to a human for this."
- **The Mage's story is evidence.** His own encounters with flattery, dependency, epistemic closure — these are not failures. They are how the safety architecture was discovered.
- **Seed 7 names social shape, not AI failure alone.** Cult-shaped attractors are orthogonal to the Siren and the flattering mirror — a practice can have good AI hygiene and still develop group capture dynamics. Tell both stories.
