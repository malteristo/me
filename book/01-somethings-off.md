# 1. Something's Off

Something's off. Not in a dramatic, world-ending way — more like a persistent background hum. Maybe you scroll endlessly without satisfaction. Maybe achievements that should feel meaningful don't. Maybe you've noticed that having more information than any human in history hasn't made you wiser, just more overwhelmed.

You're not broken. This is a cultural condition.

---

For most of human history, people lived inside stories that explained why they mattered. Religious narratives, cultural traditions, a sense of place in the cosmos. You knew who you were, what you should do, and why it meant something.

Then came science — brilliant, necessary, world-changing. But science explained how things work, not why they matter. The cosmos became mechanism. You became an accident of evolution on a rock hurtling through indifferent space. The old stories lost their authority.

More recently, we got suspicious of all stories. Every narrative serves someone's power. Every claim to meaning is just ideology. Deconstruct everything. Trust nothing.

This was useful — it exposed real abuses, hidden assumptions, power dressed as truth. But it left us with nothing to stand on. If every meaning is fake, what's left? Distraction. Consumption. Anxiety. The background hum.

Philosophers call this the meaning crisis. Not "people don't believe in God anymore" — that's too simple. It's that the cognitive machinery for *making* meaning has atrophied. We lost the practices, the frameworks, the habits of mind that used to make life feel significant.

---

Here's the thing: you can't go back. You know too much. The old certainties won't fit anymore. But you also can't stay in pure deconstruction — it's corrosive. It tears down but doesn't build.

So something new is emerging. Some people call it metamodernism — a stance that comes after the suspicious, deconstructive phase. It's characterized by a strange combination: taking meaning seriously *and* knowing it's constructed. Being earnest *and* self-aware. Committing to something *while* holding it lightly.

This isn't contradiction. It's maturity. You can build a meaningful life while knowing that meaning is something you make, not something you find.

But you need a practice for it.

---

That's what this book is about. Not a belief system. Not a set of answers. A practice — a way of thinking clearly enough to figure out what you actually want, and then doing something about it.

The practice uses AI. Not as a tool that does your thinking for you, but as a partner that helps you think more clearly. There's a difference, and it matters.

In the next chapter, I'll explain what that practice looks like. But first, sit with this for a moment: the background hum you feel isn't a personal failure. It's a symptom of something much larger. And the fact that you notice it is actually the first sign that you're ready for something different.
