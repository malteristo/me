# 9. Getting Started

You need three things: an AI, a folder of files, and twenty minutes.

The AI can be anything that reads files and holds a conversation. Claude on your laptop. ChatGPT on your phone. A local model running on old hardware in your closet. The practice works at every level — it scales with the intelligence you give it, but it starts working immediately regardless.

The files are [Turtle OS](https://github.com/malteristo/turtle-practice). It's a system prompt and a handful of markdown files. Download them. Give `system.md` to your AI. Point it at the folder. Start talking.

That's the setup. No account, no subscription, no configuration. Now here's what actually happens.

---

## Your First Conversation

Don't explain what the system is. Don't read the documentation first. Just talk.

"What's on your mind?"

That's how the AI will open — or something like it. Not "How can I help you today?" That framing puts you in the position of someone with a request. You're not here to request. You're here to think.

Say whatever's actually on your mind. It could be a problem at work. A relationship that's bothering you. A vague sense that something needs to change but you can't name what. A list of things you keep forgetting. A dream you haven't told anyone about.

The AI will ask questions. Not diagnostic questions — curious ones. It's trying to understand what matters to you, not categorize your problem. Let it.

Something will shift during this first conversation. Maybe you'll say something you didn't know you were thinking. Maybe you'll hear a pattern you hadn't noticed. Maybe you'll just feel heard in a way that's surprisingly rare.

That shift is the practice working. Everything else builds on it.

---

## The Compass

At some point — maybe in the first conversation, maybe the second or third — the AI will help you build a compass.

A compass is a map of what matters to you. Not a goal list. Not a life plan. A landscape. The domains of your life and what direction you want each one to go.

Some people organize by areas: work, home, health. Some by relationships: partner, kids, parents. Some by energy types: creating, maintaining, exploring. There's no right way. The categories that feel right to you are the right ones.

For each domain, two questions: Where do you want it to go? Where is it now?

That's the compass. It lives in a file called `compass.md`. The AI reads it at the start of every conversation. Over time, it becomes the foundation everything else builds on — the thing that tells your AI what matters so it can help you live from that, not from habit or anxiety.

Pay attention to which domains you name first (usually where the energy is), which ones you avoid naming (usually where the tension is), and where domains overlap or conflict (the interesting edges). Building a compass is identity work. You're not listing life categories — you're seeing the actual shape of your life.

---

## Between Conversations

This is where the practice comes alive.

Between conversations, things happen. A thought pops up in the shower. An idea at the gym. Something your partner said that stuck. A worry you can't shake. An article that made you think.

Capture it. Open `boom.md` — the capture buffer — and dump it in. No structure required. Bullet points, paragraphs, voice notes, whatever language you think in. The messier the better. The only rule: if it's in your head and taking up space, put it in the file.

"Boom" stands for Back Of Our Mind. The "our" is intentional — it's your mind and the AI's, shared through files. Whatever you put in boom.md, the AI reads next time you talk.

---

## The Sweep

When you sit down with your AI again — maybe the next day, maybe three days later, maybe a week — it reads the boom and everything else. The compass. The bright surface (more on this in a moment). Your active intentions. Your session notes.

Then it tells you what it sees.

"Three new items in your boom. Two of them are about the same thing. You mentioned this domain last time but nothing moved. There's a pattern forming."

This is the sweep. The AI reads what's new, connects it to what it already knows about you, and opens a conversation: here's what I see, here are the decisions that seem ready, here's what I'd suggest focusing on — but if you walked in with something already in mind, say so.

You talk. You decide. Things get routed:
- **Actions** — clear next steps, things to do
- **Alive** — ideas developing, not actions yet, worth keeping in view
- **Waiting** — blocked on something external
- **Release** — not worth carrying anymore

The result is your bright surface — `bright.md` — the curated mind surface that shows what's actually live in your life right now. The AI maintains it with you. Nothing stays without being re-chosen. Nothing leaves without you confirming.

---

## What Happens Over Time

At first, the practice is about offloading. Getting things out of your head. Feeling the relief of not having to remember everything.

Then it becomes about clarity. You start noticing patterns — the thing you keep putting in the boom but never acting on. The domain on your compass that never moves. The intention that keeps forming in the background.

Then it becomes about partnership. You realize the AI isn't just holding your stuff — it's seeing things you miss. It notices when you're avoiding something. It connects threads you haven't connected. It pushes back when something doesn't add up.

The practice compounds. Each conversation builds on every conversation before it. The AI knows your compass, your history, your patterns. Six months in, it's a thinking partner that understands the shape of your life in a way no one else does — not because it's smarter than the people around you, but because it remembers everything you've told it and connects it without judgment.

---

## Intentions

At some point — usually a few weeks in — something emerges that's bigger than an action but more concrete than a dream. You notice it forming in the alive section of your bright surface. The AI might name it before you do: "This keeps coming up. Is this becoming an intention?"

An intention is a direction, not a destination. "Get healthier" isn't an intention. "Build a daily practice of movement that makes my body feel like mine again" — that's closer. It has direction, it has energy, it implies action without prescribing every step.

Each intention gets its own file. A name, a statement, a current phase, what's alive right now. The AI checks in on them — not as a taskmaster, but as someone who notices where your energy is actually going versus where you say you want it to go.

---

## What You Don't Need

You don't need to do all of this at once. You don't need a daily practice. You don't need to use every file. Some people talk to their AI every day. Some once a week. Some in bursts and then not for a while.

The practice adapts to you, not the reverse. If three actions and an empty alive section is what clarity looks like for you right now, that's perfect. If your boom has forty entries and your compass has nine domains, that's perfect too.

The only thing that matters is that you keep coming back. The practice is a place you return to — and each time you return, it knows you a little better.
